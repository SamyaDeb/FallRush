# G4.8: the "soft toy" look (replaces the G4.8 bevel-only scope; about 5–6 h, render only)

You are the technical artist on **Fall Rush** (Unity 6000.3.28f1 URP, WebGL, code-built maps). Work in one session, alone.

## Why (the user, 2026-11-20, after playing the G4.6 build)
> "its too much colourful and unnatural type, i want designs type fall guys on top of this"

G2/G3 (`docs/proof/g4/palette-*-before.png`) was **washed out**: near-white everything. G4.6 (`palette-*-after.png`) swung to the other extreme: **huge flat, fully saturated, unshaded slabs** (an all-blue Capsule Run deck, a lime / magenta / purple Goo Tower). The genre look the user wants sits between the two. The difference is *materiality*, not hue:

| Genre look (observed, our own implementation) | G4.6 today |
|---|---|
| Big walkable surfaces calm: light pastel / cream, tone-on-tone patterns (soft stripes, dots, checks) | Big surfaces saturated and flat |
| Strong colour on **edges and objects**: padded rims, bumpers, props, hazards, characters | Strong colour everywhere, so nothing stands out |
| Rounded, chunky, inflatable / foam shapes, thick platforms | Hard thin boxes |
| Soft real shadows, ambient occlusion in corners, a soft plastic / foam sheen with highlights | No cast shadows (blob only), flat cel bands |
| Soft bright sky, big clouds, haze, a stadium / backdrop in the distance | Plain gradient, sparse clouds |

**Originality (non-negotiable):**
- Observe the genre: pacing, readability, saturation balance, soft shapes.
- **Never extract, decompile, rip or trace another game's files, textures, obstacles, layouts or UI** (REA was declined and stays declined).
- Every pattern, mesh and colour here is ours, generated in code.
- Bolt stays our capsule-toy Rushling.
- Our motif: a capsule-toy machine (capsule seams, coin slots, prize stars).

  - Running next to the G4.7 session: follow §P.

## 1. Look toggle first (so the user can compare in play)
Add a dev / render-only switch, `?look=soft|candy` (WebGL) or `-fallrushLook soft|candy` (desktop):
- **soft** is the new default;
- **candy** = G4.6 exactly.

Implement it with a palette-token set per look and shader globals. Keep it cheap: one merged mesh set, with vertex colours carrying a section index + role (top / side / rim) and the shader picking the colour from a small global palette array. If that is too invasive, bake two vertex-colour channels (uv3 = candy) instead. Write down which you chose. Pressing **F7** in-game toggles the look (dev only), so the user can A/B it on the spot.

## 2. Palette: calm surfaces, colour on the edges
- **Walkable tops:** pastel, warm-tinted, **saturation 0.12–0.28, value 0.93–0.98**; never pure white, never grey. Each section keeps its G4.6 hue *family* as a light tint:
  - Soda blue → baby blue `~#DDF1FF`;
  - Tangerine → peach cream `~#FFE8D6`;
  - Sour apple → pistachio `~#E6F7D4`;
  - Bubblegum → blush `~#FFE3EE`;
  - Grape fizz → lavender `~#ECE3FF`;
  - Lemon drop → vanilla `~#FFF6D6`.
  Tune these on screen; the hex values are a starting point.
- **Sides:** a mid tone of the same hue (saturation 0.35–0.55), darker toward the bottom (gradient).
- **Rims / trim / padding:** the full G4.6 hue (the saturated colour now lives here).
- **Hazards:** keep hot pink + ink stripe, saturated, with a gloss highlight: they must be the loudest thing on screen.
- **Goals / springs:** mint + checker. **Goo:** purple + glitter.
- **Saturation budget:** on every spawn and action screenshot, pixels with saturation > 0.6 make up **≤ 30 %** of the frame (G4.6 was far above). Measure it with a small script on the PNGs and report it per shot.

## 3. Materiality
1. **Bevelled, chunky geometry** (the original G4.8):
   - `ProcMesh` bevelled box (radius 0.15–0.25 m, 2–3 segments) and rounded cylinders with soft caps;
   - every walkable block, deck, step and platform render mesh at least 0.6 m thick (visually: extend downward only, never above or beyond the collider top edge);
   - keep the G2.4 static-chunk merge so draw calls stay ≤ 150.
2. **Padded rims:** every platform edge gets a rounded bumper strip (a soft tube or bevelled lip, about 0.2–0.3 m) in the section's saturated hue, inside the collider footprint or within 0.15 m. This is the main carrier of colour.
3. **Tone-on-tone surface patterns** in `Toon.shader`, procedural in world space, no textures:
   - wide soft stripes, polka dots, checks or chevrons, one pattern per section;
   - contrast only ±5–8 % value, anti-aliased with `fwidth`;
   - they must not compete with hazard stripes or the belts' chevron light.
4. **Soft "foam / vinyl" shading:**
   - a wider, softer cel ramp (or a smooth half-Lambert blended 50 % with the bands);
   - a broad soft specular highlight (gloss) on rims, props and hazards;
   - a Fresnel sky tint on edges;
   - vertex AO stronger in corners and under lips;
   - a bottom-dark gradient on every prop (in the plan already).
5. **Real soft shadows from the sun:**
   - URP main-light shadows: one cascade, distance about 50–70 m, soft, resolution 1024–2048;
   - casters are players, hazards, props and platforms;
   - keep the blob shadow under Rushlings (it reads the landing spot).
   Measure the WebGL cost. If it breaks 60 FPS / 1 % low on High, use shadows on High only and keep blob + AO on Low. Never ship worse than budget.
6. **Grading, back toward natural:**
   - saturation about **+6**, contrast about **+8**, exposure **0** (the G4.6 grade pushed everything too far);
   - Neutral tonemapping if WebGL supports the HDR path (G4.7 is checking this: read its result);
   - a slight warm sun `#FFF3E0`, a cool sky ambient, a soft bloom only on gloss highlights (threshold ≥ 1.2).
7. **Sky and backdrop** (a light slice of G4.10, render only):
   - a softer sky gradient with a hazy pale horizon;
   - bigger, fluffier cloud clusters below and around;
   - distance fog in the horizon colour;
   - a simple far stadium silhouette (bleacher shapes, a few banners and flags, inflatable towers), low-poly, merged, one material.
